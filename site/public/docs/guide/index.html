Building an app

Building an app in Cairn, one chapter at a time, for someone who has never seen it.

This guide walks through one idea at a time, mostly on two small apps from the repository: Counter, a count with an Increment and a Reset button, and Clock, a stopwatch that talks to the outside world. Every code sample is the real source, included by the docs build, so what you read is what compiles.

You will need to read some Lean 4. You don't need to be good at proofs: most of the proofs an app needs are one line, and the guide shows where the longer ones come from.

Chapters

  1. Your first app: what a Cairn app is made of, running Counter, and starting an app of your own.
  2. Moves and flows: declaring what a person can do, and the paths through it.
  3. The view and layout: what the app shows, as a tree of boxes laid out in whole points.
  4. Proving a claim: the proofs a move needs, and the ones that reach the screen.
  5. Effects and the host: time, timers, files and other requests, and subscriptions.
  6. Shipping: checking, bundling and signing an app, and publishing its map.

What Cairn is for

Most UI frameworks let you say what an app looks like. Cairn also asks you to say what it does, in a form a machine can check. Pressing ^2 in Counter "sets the count to zero, and Reset disappears": that sentence sits in the source next to a Lean statement of it and a proof, and the app doesn't compile without the proof. The id ^2 means Reset for as long as the app exists, so a bug report, a test or a review can cite it.

To make those claims mean something, the code they talk about has to be the code that runs. So everything a person can observe is computed in Lean, and the Swift host only carries events in and pixels out.

Cairn is research, and these docs grow with it. The code samples are included from the source that make check builds.
