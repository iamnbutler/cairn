The ladder

A claim shows every kind of evidence it has. It reads as the strongest of them, so a proved invariant reads as proved whatever else ran. Strongest first:

  1. provedEvery theorem the claim names (each move handler's sound; a flow's live and reaches; an invariant's init, step and reachable) is in lake exe audit --json and uses only propext, Classical.choice and Quot.sound. make check also replays every declaration through the kernel.
  2. fuzzed NN random sequences of the Lean model and runtime (make fuzz, docs/fuzz.md), with each invariant's check, presence and pressability after every step. For an assumption, the trusted-base fuzzer (build/fuzz/trusted.json) found no counterexample.
  3. tested NThe running app, driven through its debug socket by generated sequences (make conformance, docs/conformance.md), matched the model's frame, accessibility tree, effects and pixels after every step. N counts the steps that exercised a move.
  4. seenA headless capture of the app's own frames shows the control (for a move), the whole witness performed on the controls it names (for a flow), or the place (for a place). Windows and panels are drawn by the real renderer; a status item's menu is drawn by the capture from the NSMenu the host built.
  5. assumedAn assume: stated, never proved. Theorems that rely on it take it as a hypothesis.

Two more readings: broken when any evidence failed (a theorem missing or using another axiom, a fuzz or conformance failure, or a capture that contradicts a live witness), and untested when a live claim has no evidence at all. A dashed chip, not tested, is evidence the claim could have and doesn't: a gap.

Proved and fuzzed are about the Lean model; tested and seen are about the app. A proof is the stronger statement, but only conformance reaches through the host, so a claim is fully covered when it has every chip its kind allows: a move can be proved, fuzzed, tested and seen; a flow proved, tested and seen; an invariant proved, fuzzed and tested; a place seen.
